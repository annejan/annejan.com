The AI key

On your own NPU

annejan.com

Your laptop has an AI key. Make it do something, on your own NPU.

New laptops have a Copilot key. On Linux it does nothing. local-llm-hotkeys gives it four jobs on KDE Plasma, and runs all of them on the AMD Ryzen AI NPU that is already in the laptop. There is no cloud, no account and no subscription. Nothing leaves your computer, it works offline, and the GPU stays free.

Four jobs for one key

  • Copilot

    Chat

    A chat window. Ask anything, then ask follow-up questions. It knows your distribution and desktop, so the commands fit your system.

  • Alt Copilot

    Translate

    The copied text, translated to your language. A Ukrainian article, a German invoice, a Japanese error message.

  • Ctrl Copilot

    Explain

    The copied text in a few plain sentences, in your language, with a to-do list if it asks you to do something.

  • Ctrl Alt Copilot

    What is on my screen?

    The window in front of you, explained: "what is this error, and what do I do?" Then ask on.

For example

  • A letter from the tax office that you do not understand: copy it, press Ctrl Copilot, and read what it says and what you must do before which date.
  • An error dialog, a settings page, a chart: press Ctrl Alt Copilot, then ask "and how do I fix that?"
  • An email to someone who does not read your language: press Meta Ctrl Shift D, speak your language, and the English text is typed for you.

No AI key? These work too

Meta Shift Qchat
Meta Shift Ttranslate the clipboard
Meta Shift Eexplain the clipboard
Meta Shift Wexplain the active window
Meta Shift Xexplain a screen region that you select
Meta Shift Ytranslate a screen region (OCR)
Meta Shift Ffix spelling and grammar of the clipboard
Meta Shift Ddictate: speak, and the text is typed
Meta Ctrl Shift Ddictate and translate

In Dolphin, right-click a document to translate it or have it explained.

Install

git clone https://github.com/annejan/local-llm-hotkeys.git
cd local-llm-hotkeys
./install.sh

install.sh needs no root. It checks what is missing and prints the install command for openSUSE, Debian, Ubuntu, Fedora or Arch, then sets up the commands, the hotkeys, the Dolphin menu and the key map for the Copilot key. The models run in FastFlowLM on the NPU; on a computer without one, ollama on a GPU works too.

The README has every command and setting. Release files are signed; the README shows how to check them.
